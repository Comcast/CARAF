Modern infrastructures, systems, and services must adopt [Post‑Quantum Cryptography (PQC) algorithms](https://www.nist.gov/cybersecurity/what-post-quantum-cryptography) in response to the emerging quantum computing threat. Large‑scale quantum computers have the potential to break widely deployed classical cryptographic algorithms, undermining the confidentiality, integrity, and authenticity of data and systems.

There are two main classes of PQC algorithms relevant to industry adoption (as standardized by NIST in 2024: [NIST PQC standards](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2024/08/nist-releases-first-3-finalized-post-quantum-encryption-standards)):
- **Digital signature algorithms**
- **Key Encapsulation Mechanisms (KEMs)**

Third‑party products and services frequently handle sensitive data, depend on cryptographic trust anchors, or integrate directly into enterprise platforms and operational environments. As a result, a third party’s readiness to support PQC has a direct impact on an organization’s overall security posture and long‑term resilience.

This guide supports **third‑party risk assessment for PQC** and is intended for security, risk, and procurement stakeholders who engage external vendors, suppliers, or service providers whose offerings may remain in use beyond 2030, when widespread PQC migration is expected to be necessary.

PQC readiness should be evaluated during the **inherent risk assessment** phase of a third‑party engagement and used as an input to the organization’s third‑party risk management and security assurance processes, including more detailed third-party security assessments where appropriate.
